      Compounds: who can do which job

      A setup is a compound: a model, plus the route or the machine and runtime, plus a program where one is needed. The same model in a different compound behaves differently. Click one to light up its elements in the table.

      Why it is laid out this way

      • The real table works because both axes are causes: a row is a shell, a column is the outer electrons. Position alone tells you how an element behaves.
      • Here a row is custody. Above the dashed line you can hold the weights, so no service can gate them. Below it the lab holds them and decides.
      • A column is lineage: one lab's training, licence and jurisdiction. Columns run from labs that publish everything to labs that publish nothing, which is why the filled cells step down to the right.
      • Down any column the models get stronger and the gate gets tighter. Across any row you see what each lab offers for the same hardware.
      • Versions are isotopes: the same element, a different weight. Older isotopes are usually looser.
      • A setup is a compound, not an element. The same model in a different compound behaves differently, which is why routes, machines and programs are elements here too.

      How to read it

      • A local shell is the smallest machine that holds the model at 4-bit, at roughly 0.55 GB per billion parameters. Sizes come from press and model lists.
      • Read: the vendor's page or the report itself. Told: press or a search summary. Yours: your records or your words. By custody: it follows from who holds the weights. Assumed: ordinary coding, with no source saying otherwise. Predicted: taken from a sourced neighbour.
      • "No service gate" is not "the model says yes". Open models from US labs are trained to refuse a good deal. A test on your own machine settles it and touches no account.
      • An empty cell is a lab with nothing in that shell. A dashed tile is a gap the pattern predicts.
      • Left out: Amazon, and labs outside the US and China, which drops Mistral, FLUX and LTX. A media tool with nothing sourced about it is left off, not shown as unknown.
