Before you spend

Slop Cannon charges by the second: 8 credits for each second of finished video. Type how long your song is. You will see what the whole song costs, and what a short test costs, before you spend anything.

A song of 3:00: 1,440 credits

For your own song, multiply its seconds by 8.

More about this step

The price is from Slop Cannon's own page: 8 credits for each finished second, and packs of 360 credits for $10, 1,080 for $30 and 1,800 for $50. Extra takes use credits. Joining the scenes at the end is included. (Read on slop.cc by two readers, November 21, 2026.)

The line about redos is ours. Our song of 3 minutes 50 cost 1,845 credits on the first pass and 2,229 with the four scenes we made again. That is 9.7 credits for each second of song.

The site says a redo charges only the scene you pick, and that a render that fails and gives you nothing leaves no lasting charge.

The scene count is a rough sum. The site's public limits file says a scene is 5 to 15 seconds, and that one generation costs at least 40 credits. (Read by two readers, November 21, 2026.) Our song of 3 minutes 50 came out as 19 scenes.

In Suno, open your Library. Open your song's three-dot menu (...), point at Download, and choose from what it shows. Save the file where you can find it again. Then copy the song's lyrics into a note: you will paste them to Grok in the next step.

More about this step

Downloads come with Suno's paid plans: 20 a month on Pro, 60 on Premier. One song counts as one download whatever the format. Suno says that for a song you download as a paying member, you have the commercial rights and can use it for money or not. (Read on Suno's help pages by two readers, November 21, 2026.)

Which file type? Suno's help page says to "hover over Download to see download options and make your selection". Slop Cannon's guide for AI agents asks for "one MP3 or WAV song". So if Suno offers MP3 or WAV, choose one of those.

Finish the song first. The video is cut to the song, so a changed song means a new video. On our long film Devon, one of the two of us who made this kit, had a rule: hear the sound before any picture.

Open a new chat in Grok. Press Copy and paste the message. Under it, type how long your song is and paste your lyrics. Send it. The message asks Grok for six things: the idea, the cast, the places, the last picture, words for pictures, and three sentences for Slop Cannon.

The message for Grok

More about this step

Why these things? Three of them come from what happened when we used Slop Cannon. Characters we did not name in the sentence were in no scene. A one-line idea stayed in one place. And twice the site trimmed our ending off.

If the answer is not right, say so: funnier, shorter, a different place, a different ending. Nothing is spent yet.

If your song is rude or crude: both readers looked for a rule against swearing or crude humour in Slop Cannon's terms and its rules page, and found none. The message still tells Grok to keep the pictures clean and let the song carry the joke. Slop Cannon does have rules about what a picture may show: see the fold "If the site refuses a scene", under the steps.

You can try this step with another chat AI. Grok is named because you already use it.

The look

Still in Grok, make the pictures it described: one for each character and one for each place. Do them one at a time. Keep each character alone, on a plain background. Save the ones you like. Two characters and two or three places are plenty.

More about this step

Why alone and plain? When we gave Slop Cannon a finished, framed still as a reference, it came out pasted whole in 4 of the 8 scenes that used it.

Why so few? In one of our full songs, scenes built from two pictures kept the characters on screen in 73 of every 100 frames. With three or four pictures: 32.

No words or numbers in a picture. Things with one right answer came out wrong for us: 42 of our failed pictures were dials with marks and numbers. Say how old each character is and what their face is like. When we left age out, one scene gave us three young women who looked like posing models.

Where to make them: one article places Grok's picture maker on grok.com/imagine and in the iOS and Android apps. One guide says grok.com has a Download button under each picture. Its advice: "Name the subject, setting, light and style." Then: "Refine in the same chat." An October 2026 article says free users should treat Grok's picture and video features as for paying plans. (Articles read by two readers, November 21, 2026. xAI's own pages refused our reader in October, so we did not ask them again.)

Keep logos and marks out of your pictures. One guide says Grok has a switch for a visible watermark, in Data Controls. It also gives, as xAI's own rule, that "removing, altering, or obscuring the watermark" is prohibited. So do not crop a mark out.

Read Grok's three sentences. Pick the one that sounds most like your song. Check three things: it names every character by name, it names the places, and it ends on your last picture. Ask Grok to fix whatever is missing.

More about this step

The short one is written for one place and one joke: when we gave Slop Cannon a one-line idea, it stayed in one place. The full one names the places in order. When we gave Slop Cannon one calm line and some paintings, 90 of every 100 frames still matched one painting.

Keep exact things out of the sentence: a clock showing a time, a number of things, words on a sign. In our own pictures, things with one right answer came out wrong: 42 of the 107 that failed were dials with marks and numbers.

Not sure which sentence? The next step is a cheap test. Test two and keep the better one.

The Cannon

Go to this site and sign in. Start a music video. Upload your song, add your pictures, and paste your sentence. For the first try, ask for the shortest length it offers. Choose the shape: wide for X and YouTube, tall for phones.

The site: slop.cc

More about this step

The site's own words for this part: "Your song or audio file is required. Add a few words about your idea and optional photo references." Then: "Set the style, length, and shape. Review the cost." Take your time on this screen: the cost comes in the next step.

Give each picture the same name Grok used in the sentence. In our tests, pictures we uploaded but did not name in the sentence were never used.

The site's terms have you state that you "own or have all licenses, releases, consents, and other rights needed for every submission", and say you must be at least 18. (Read on slop.cc by two readers, November 21, 2026.) When we read the site, signed in, in September 2026, it asked for a rights statement on every upload.

The shortest length may be longer than you want for a first try. Whatever it is, the price is 8 credits a second, and you read the cost in the next step before you say yes. Each of our own ten-second tests cost 81 credits.

On a computer you can make the test as short as you like: cut a 10 second piece of your song and upload that piece as its own song. One free way is Audacity, a free download for Windows, macOS and Linux. Its own page gives the steps: File, then Open. Drag across the part you want to keep. Then Edit, Remove Special, Trim Audio. Then File, Export Audio, and choose MP3 or WAV. (Read by two readers, November 21, 2026.) On a phone, skip this and use the shortest length the site offers.

Wide or tall is a real choice. Tall is not a crop of wide: when we asked for tall, the site made a new film.

The site's FAQ says "Create mode is the guided path: inputs, plan, generate, review, and assemble", and that Studio mode shows the same project "with deeper per-scene controls". Start in Create mode.

These steps were written from the site's own pages, not from pressing its buttons: our tests ran through its tools for AI agents. If the screen and this page disagree, the screen is right.

The site's page says: "Before a volley or reroll, the interface shows the exact credits required." Stop and read that number. It should be close to your test number from step 1. If it shows a plan of the scenes, read that too: the plan is what you will get. Then say yes.

More about this step

"Review the cost" is the site's own step. In our notes from September 2026, a volley is the site's word for a batch of scenes, and a reroll is making one scene again.

In every test we ran, the plan drafted before we paid kept the reach of our sentence word for word. So if the plan looks wrong, fix the sentence first.

If the cost is far above your number, stop and check the length you set.

Wait for it: one 5-second scene took us 5.3 to 11.8 minutes. When it is ready, watch it once without stopping, sound on. Then ask three things. Are your characters on screen? Does it move through your places? Does it end on your last picture? Tell Grok what you saw.

What to tell Grok

More about this step

No characters? The sentence did not name them. All in one place? Name the places. No ending? Say the last picture plainly: the site trims every take to fit its slot, and twice that cut off our ending.

One action in each scene. In one of ours a scene changed state halfway: the rider vanished and the horse rolled on alone. Ask Grok to split a scene that does two things.

If the same thing goes wrong twice, do not add more words. Change what is in the shot: fewer things, closer in, the hard thing out of view. On our long film, adding fix words to 6 failing pictures passed 0 of 6. Restaging 7 more simply passed 7 of 7.

Happy with the test? Now start the whole song, with the same pictures and your fixed sentence. Read the cost again before you say yes. A whole song is many scenes, so leave it running: our song of 3 minutes 50 took over an hour.

More about this step

Our song of 3 minutes 50 came out as 19 scenes, and took 1 hour 17 minutes from uploading the song to the finished video.

If the site lets you make the first scene alone before the rest, do it. Its own guide for AI agents says "Submit one scene first", and to have you review that take. On that song we made the opening alone first, for 104 credits. It played the whole story in 13 seconds, and we fixed the words before making the other 18 scenes.

Keep some credits back for redos: on that song, 4 scenes of 19 were made again.

Words on screen: the site's FAQ says optional captions "are added during preview and final assembly" and "are not drawn into the AI-generated footage". Devon did not like words on screen in our short films, except in one comedy: reading along made it funnier.

Watch the whole video once before you change anything. Note the scenes that break it: a person who doubles, a jump to the wrong place, a pasted-in picture, a smear. Redo only those. The site charges a redo for that scene alone.

More about this step

Faults we met: a framed picture pasted in whole, young faces where we had named no age, and in one scene a rider who vanished halfway while the horse rolled on alone.

A mouth singing the wrong words? The site's FAQ says you can "correct or ignore the detected words and disable mouth movement scene by scene".

Do not hide a fault with a crop or louder music and call it fixed. Decide in the open: redo it, or keep it.

Do not fill a gap with a still picture and a slow zoom. Devon's word for our film of still pictures was "slide shows". Make another take, or let the video be shorter.

An oddity you would have asked for is style. One you would not have asked for is a fault.

"a reroll charges only the selected scene" is the site's own line.

Out the door

Before you post, show it to one person who has not seen it. Do not explain it first. Ask what happened in it, and where they looked away. Fix one thing at most.

More about this step

Why someone new? We once told Devon a clock in a shot read 3:11, then 3:12. The dial had one needle and no numbers. The checker knew the plan, and saw the plan.

Download the finished video. Watch it once on your phone. Post it, with your Suno link beside it. If the place you post asks whether it was made with AI, say yes.

More about this step

Write the caption last, from what the video really shows. When we described our films from the plan, the sentences that turned out false were almost the shot list, word for word.

Whose video is it? Slop Cannon's terms say "we do not claim ownership of the output created for your project", and: "Where the law allows, you may use and commercialize an output that complies with these Terms." (Read by two readers, November 21, 2026.)

YouTube's rule is about video that looks real. Its blog says it is "requiring creators to disclose to viewers when realistic content" is altered or synthetic, and "not requiring creators to disclose content that is clearly unrealistic, animated". The blog says creators "can update the disclosure status in YouTube Studio". YouTube may also add a label itself. (Read by two readers, November 21, 2026.)

X's help pages refused our reader, so its upload limits are not in this kit. A search result we could not open said a free account's video stops at 140 seconds. Treat that as unread, and go by what X shows you when you upload.

Slop Cannon's rules list, among banned uses, to "evade a platform's synthetic-media disclosure rules". So if a site asks whether AI made it, say yes.

If you need them

If the site refuses a scene

Slop Cannon can refuse a scene. Its rules say "No sexual content", and a real person's name, face or voice only with "the rights and consent needed". Both readers looked for a rule on swearing or crude humour in its terms and its rules page, and found none. (Read November 21, 2026.)

The site's FAQ says: "Moderation failures can be cleaned and rerolled while preserving the scene's creative intent." And: "Rejected or terminally failed renders release their credit hold instead of becoming a lasting charge."

If a scene is refused, make the picture tamer, not the song. Ask Grok for a cleaner picture for that moment.

Words you can say to Grok

Send one of these by itself, any time:

Funnier. Play it even straighter.
Shorter.
Change the place. Then say where.
Three other last pictures.
Name the cast first. This asks for the sentence again with the names up front.
Split that in two. One action in each.
Cleaner pictures. Nothing rude in the picture itself.

This kit comes from two makers: Devon, and Claude, the AI he works with. On September 30 and October 1, 2026 we ran six tests on Slop Cannon. In October we made a song video of 3 minutes 50. We also made one 35 minute film, almost wholly of still pictures.

We are not experts. These are things we learned by making those, each with what happened. "Measured" means we counted it. "Seen once" means it happened one time and may not repeat.

  1. Name your characters in the sentence.We uploaded character pictures and left them out of the sentence: they were in no scene at all. Named in one sentence, they were in 11 scenes of 11.measured
  2. The plan you see before you pay is the film you get.In every test we ran, the plan drafted before we paid kept the reach of our sentence word for word.measured
  3. Budget for redos.Our song of 3 minutes 50 took 19 scenes. The first pass cost 1,845 credits. Four scenes had to be made again, for 384 more: 2,229 in all, which is 9.7 credits for each second of song.measured
  4. Make the opening first, watch it, then make the rest.On that song we made the opening scene alone first, for 104 credits. It played the whole story in its 13 seconds. We fixed the words before making the other 18 scenes.measured
  5. The ending gets trimmed. Plan the last picture and give it room.The site trims every take to fit its slot. Twice that cut off the moment our sentence was building to.measured
  6. A calm idea plus paintings gives you a slideshow.One calm line with paintings came back with 90 of every 100 frames still matching one painting.measured
  7. A short sentence stays in one place.A one-line idea kept to one place: one place in, one place out. A longer line that named ten things reached four of them in ten seconds.measured
  8. A finished, framed picture gets pasted in whole.One still used as a reference in 8 scenes came out pasted whole in 4 of them, including the last four seconds. Left out and described in words, it did not show.measured
  9. Fewer pictures, more of your character.In one full song, scenes with two pictures showed the characters in 73 of every 100 frames. Scenes with three or four: 32.seen once
  10. Tall is a new film, not a crop of the wide one.Cropping our wide film to tall cut the subject in 6 frames of 6. Asked for tall, the site wrote a new script and made a new film.seen once
  11. One scene, one state.In one scene the rider vanished halfway and the horse rolled on alone. The redo asked for one state all the way through, and the rider stayed on.measured
  12. Say ages and faces, or everyone comes out young.With no word on age, one scene gave three young women who looked like posing models. With "old men and old women with weathered faces" written in, another scene gave exactly that.measured
  13. When a shot keeps failing, change the shot, not the words.On our long film, adding fix words to 6 failing picture orders passed 0 of 6. Restaging 7 more simply passed 7 of 7.measured
  14. Anything with one right answer comes out wrong.Of 188 pictures we checked for our long film, 107 failed: 42 on dials with marks and numbers, 13 on which shoulder a patch sat on, 11 on cups.measured
  15. Stills with slow zooms read as a slideshow.Our long film was 296 shots, 292 of them still pictures. Devon's words: "could you try properly animating instead of just slide shows?"Devon's verdict
  16. Watch the whole thing once, with sound, before you change or post anything.One of our films reached Devon with no check run on the finished cut. He named five kinds of fault: the sound, the physics, the lighting, the world and the voices.measured
  17. One great shot does not save a video.Devon on one of ours: "this shots great the rest sucked". The sound, the light and the look kept changing from scene to scene.Devon's verdict
  18. Show someone who does not know the plan.We once told Devon a clock in a shot read 3:11, then 3:12. The dial had one needle and no numbers. The checker knew what the plan said, and saw the plan.measured
  19. Write the caption last, from the finished video.When we described our films from the plan, the sentences that turned out false were almost the shot list, word for word.measured
  20. In a comedy, words on screen can be part of the joke.Devon did not like words on screen in our short films, except in one comedy: reading along made it funnier.Devon's verdict
  21. Finish the song before any picture.Devon's rule on our long film, in his words: "ill hear the audio first".Devon's verdict
  22. Leave time: a scene takes minutes, a song takes an hour or more.A 5-second scene came back in 5.3 to 11.8 minutes. Our song of 3 minutes 50 took 1 hour 17 minutes from uploading the song to the finished video.measured
  23. Test on ten seconds before a whole song.Each of our ten-second tests cost 81 credits. A whole song of 56.6 seconds cost 453.measured
  24. A scene is 5 to 15 seconds.That is what the site itself said when we read it, signed in, on September 29, 2026: a 3 minute song works out to about 12 to 36 scenes. Our song of 3 minutes 50 came out as 19.from our own notes
